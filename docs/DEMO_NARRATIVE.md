# GradeAI Demo Narrative

Use this as a short walkthrough for demos, assessor reviews, or pilot conversations.

The goal is not to show every feature. The goal is to show that the product has:

- clear role boundaries
- believable academic workflows
- AI used in a controlled way
- human review where trust matters

## Core Story

GradeAI is an academic assessment workflow platform.

It helps three groups:

- lecturers manage assignments, review submissions, and release results
- students understand released feedback and act on it
- admins monitor institutional reporting and governance signals

The product is deliberately role-based. Lecturers do teaching work. Students see released outcomes and support tools. Admins see oversight and reporting views.

AI supports grading, explanation, and pattern detection, but it does not replace lecturer judgement. Lecturers remain responsible for review, moderation, approval, and release.

## Demo Order

Show the product in this order:

1. Lecturer workflow
2. Student feedback experience
3. Admin oversight and reporting

This order works because it starts with the operational workflow, then shows the student outcome, then finishes with institution-level oversight.

## 1. Lecturer Workflow

Start on the lecturer overview page.

What to say:

- "This dashboard is designed around what needs attention now."
- "The aim is to help a lecturer see backlog, risk, and workflow state quickly."
- "The top section points to the next action, and the lower sections support that decision with live stats and workflow detail."

Then move through these pages:

- Overview
- Assignments
- Moderation
- Cohort Analytics
- Performance Trends

What to highlight:

- assignments, submissions, and grading status are visible in one teaching workspace
- workflow stages make it clear where submissions are sitting
- moderation and integrity checks sit inside the lecturer workflow rather than outside it
- performance views help identify where support is needed

What not to overclaim:

- do not say the AI is grading autonomously
- do not say analytics replace lecturer judgement
- do not imply every lecturer view is predictive or institution-grade reporting

Safe phrasing:

- "AI helps generate grading support and explanations."
- "Lecturers review and release final outcomes."
- "These views are meant to reduce backlog and improve visibility, not automate academic judgement."

## 2. Student Feedback Experience

Move to the student side next.

Suggested pages:

- My Grades
- Explain My Grade
- Improvement Plan

What to say:

- "The student side is focused on understanding released results and knowing what to do next."
- "Students only see grades once they have been reviewed and released."
- "Feedback is broken down so it is easier to interpret than a single block of comments."

What to highlight:

- released grade summary
- rubric breakdown
- strongest areas and areas to improve
- improvement tasks and progress

What not to overclaim:

- do not imply students can see internal moderation or unreleased grades
- do not imply the explanation tools are giving official academic decisions

Safe phrasing:

- "This helps students interpret feedback that has already been reviewed and released."
- "The system supports reflection and improvement, but the released lecturer decision remains the formal outcome."

## 3. Admin Oversight And Reporting

Finish on the admin side.

Suggested pages:

- Admin Dashboard
- Institutional Insights
- Accreditation
- External Examiner

What to say:

- "These pages are intentionally separated from lecturer teaching workflow."
- "They are read-only oversight and reporting views."
- "The aim is to support governance, quality review, and institutional reporting without leaking those views into day-to-day marking."

What to highlight:

- institutional reporting is admin-only
- the reports are data-backed from real workflow data
- external examiner export is governed and scoped
- admin CTAs stay inside oversight-safe routes

What not to overclaim:

- do not claim formal regulatory validation
- do not present pilot metrics as external accreditation approval

Safe phrasing:

- "This is an internal reporting and governance surface."
- "It helps staff review readiness and evidence, but it is not a substitute for formal institutional processes."

## Trust Boundary Summary

Keep this explanation short and consistent:

- AI can assist with grading support, explanation, and pattern detection
- lecturers remain responsible for approval and release
- students only see released outcomes
- admins oversee reporting and governance, not teaching actions

If someone asks whether the system is fully automated, the answer should be no.

## Two-Minute Version

If time is short, use this:

1. Lecturer overview and assignments
- show what needs attention now
- show grading and moderation workflow

2. Student grades
- show released feedback, rubric breakdown, and next-step support

3. Admin reports
- show institutional reporting is separate and read-only

Close with:

- "The main design decision in GradeAI is controlled assistance. AI can help structure and surface information, but people still own academic decisions."

## Common Questions

### What problem is the product solving?

It brings assignment management, AI-assisted grading support, moderation, student feedback, and institutional reporting into one role-aware workflow.

### Why separate lecturer and admin reporting views?

Because teaching workflow and institutional governance are not the same job. Keeping them separate reduces clutter and respects least-privilege access.

### Where does the student benefit show up?

In clearer released feedback, rubric-level explanation, and actionable improvement support.

### What makes this a pilot rather than a finished platform?

The product already supports the main workflows, but it should still be presented as a controlled pilot with clear boundaries rather than a fully validated institutional system.

## Final Advice

During the demo:

- show fewer pages, but explain them clearly
- use real workflow language, not buzzwords
- be explicit about where human control remains
- avoid claiming more certainty or validation than the system has earned

That makes the product feel more trustworthy and more mature.
